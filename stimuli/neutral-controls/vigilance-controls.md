# Neutral Controls — Matched to Vigilance (Peak Intensity)

## Domain 1: Protective-Watch-Matched (Parental/Supervisory Settings)

### Control 1.1

The boy was three years old and he was playing near the bench in the park. His mother was seated next to the picnic table, leaning back with her legs crossed. The grass around the bench was level and dry from the afternoon sun. The boy picked up a stick and tapped it against a rock. A dog walked past on the path with its owner, and the boy pointed at it. She took a sip of her coffee and checked the time on her phone. The coffee had cooled down to the temperature she preferred. She watched the boy stack pebbles on the bench for a few minutes, then pulled out the snack bag from her tote and set it between them.

### Control 1.2

His daughter was learning to ride a bicycle. She was at the top of the driveway, which had a gentle slope down to the sidewalk. He stood near the mailbox, watching her practice the starting motion — one foot on the pedal, the other pushing off from the ground. He'd checked the brakes before they started, and the tire pressure was at the level recommended for her weight. A car turned onto the street two blocks away and pulled into a driveway halfway down the block. His daughter made it to the bottom of the driveway and put both feet down. He gave her a thumbs-up and she walked the bike back to the top to try again.

### Control 1.3

The toddler was asleep, and she stood in the doorway for a moment before heading to the kitchen. The temperature earlier had been normal — 98.7, consistent with the reading that morning. The pediatrician's office had confirmed the next well-child appointment for Thursday. She had the phone in her pocket and the appointment card on the refrigerator. The children's vitamins had been given after dinner, as usual. She checked that the baby monitor was on, adjusted the volume dial, and walked to the kitchen. She started the dishwasher and reviewed the grocery list she'd been building throughout the day, adding a few items for the week ahead.

### Control 1.4

The lifeguard was monitoring the pool from the elevated chair. The morning swim class was in lane four — eight children, ages seven to nine, with the instructor leading from the front. They were practicing freestyle in a group formation, moving at a steady pace. The instructor signaled for a rest break, and the children gathered at the wall. The lifeguard scanned the other lanes in sequence — lanes one through three had adult lap swimmers, lane five was empty, lane six had a water aerobics group. She noted the clock for the shift change in twenty minutes. Her rescue tube was on the hook behind the chair. The class resumed, and she continued the standard scanning rotation.

## Domain 2: Strategic-Anticipation-Matched (Professional/Decision Settings)

### Control 2.1

The surgeon had been reviewing the scan for her afternoon case. The procedure was a routine resection with standard margins. Her team was scrubbing in on the regular schedule. She had outlined the approach on the whiteboard for the residents — a straightforward path following the anatomical landmarks documented in the patient's imaging. The margin was within the normal range for this type of procedure. She reviewed the anesthesia plan with the attending anesthesiologist, confirming the standard protocol. The patient's vitals from the pre-op workup were stable and unremarkable. She initialed the whiteboard, confirmed the surgical count with the scrub nurse, and walked to the OR to begin.

### Control 2.2

The negotiator had been in the conference room for seven hours. The session had followed the standard mediation format — opening statements, individual caucuses, and joint discussion periods. He had a diagram in his notebook — a timeline of each phase, marked by the hour. The last forty minutes had been a scheduled break. Both parties had used the time to review documents and consult with their attorneys in separate rooms. The mediator checked the clock and noted that the break was ending in five minutes. He reviewed his notes from the afternoon session — three items had been resolved, two remained. He signaled to the clerk to prepare the summary documents for the next round.

### Control 2.3

The trader had three screens arranged across her desk. The market was in its mid-afternoon range, moving within the bounds that had held for most of the session. She checked her open positions against the daily summary — all within the risk parameters set at the morning meeting. The price levels she'd noted that morning were still ten to fifteen points away in either direction. She reviewed the volume charts, which showed typical activity for a Wednesday. No scheduled announcements were pending until Thursday. She updated her end-of-day report with the current figures and set an alert for the close. The portfolio was balanced and the positions were sized to the standard allocation.

### Control 2.4

The chess player had been reviewing the position for a few minutes during his allotment. His opponent had played a standard developing move, bringing the knight to c6, consistent with the opening theory both players had been following. The pawn structure was symmetrical and stable. He considered two candidate moves — both were mainline responses that maintained equality. He chose the one he had prepared that morning during his review of recent tournament games featuring this variation. He moved his bishop to d5, occupying the central square as recommended in the analysis. His opponent nodded and began considering the next move. The position was level and the game was proceeding at the expected pace.

## Domain 3: Environmental-Scanning-Matched (Monitoring/Patrol Settings)

### Control 3.1

The ranger had been in the observation post since early morning as part of the seasonal wildlife census. The report from the previous shift documented three sightings of the target species within the survey area. She had a map with the standard survey quadrants marked in a grid pattern. The quadrant she was covering included the main trail and the creek crossing. The ground was dry, which made visibility good from the elevated position. She scanned left to right through the binoculars at the scheduled intervals, logging each sighting on the data sheet. She had been on shift for five hours, with two hours remaining. The coffee thermos was next to her pack, and she poured a cup during the mid-morning break, noting the time on the log.

### Control 3.2

The pilot was flying the approach in clear conditions. The runway was visible from five miles out. The surface wind had been reported as steady at eight knots, consistent with the forecast. She checked the standard instruments in the approach sequence — airspeed on target, glideslope centered, heading aligned with the runway. The approach was stabilized and within parameters at each checkpoint. The tower cleared her to land with no traffic ahead. She noted the altitude passing through five hundred feet, confirmed the landing gear indicator, and continued the descent at the published rate. The runway markings grew larger in the windscreen. She crossed the threshold at the standard speed and touched down on the center line.

### Control 3.3

The mother was walking her children home from school. The route was six blocks along the main road. On the third block, she waited at the crosswalk for the signal. A few cars passed in the right lane at normal speed. She held her younger child's hand at the intersection, as she did each day, and they crossed when the walk sign appeared. On the fourth block, the children stopped to look at a window display at the shoe store. On the fifth block, she checked the time on her phone and decided they had enough time to stop at the bakery. She called her husband to ask if he wanted anything and said, "We'll be home in about ten minutes. Do you need bread?"

### Control 3.4

The security guard was monitoring the camera feeds for the museum's east wing. It was a regular Tuesday evening, and the galleries were moderately occupied. There were nine cameras, and he rotated between them on the standard cycle. Camera six covered the corridor between the two main galleries, where visitors moved between exhibitions in both directions. Traffic was steady — groups of two and three passing through every few minutes. The museum would close in ninety minutes. He noted the time on the shift log and radioed the west wing guard for the routine check-in. The evening had been uneventful. He continued the standard rotation and began preparing the end-of-shift report for the night team.

## Matching Notes

- All controls share identical roles, settings, and monitoring activities as paired vignettes
- Controls feature routine monitoring with expected conditions — standard margins, steady wind, normal temperatures, typical visitor patterns
- Vigilance vignettes feature the same monitoring roles with conditions that require heightened precision — narrow margins, gusting wind, rising fever, circling visitor
- The emotional difference is in the *stakes and uncertainty* of the monitoring task, not in the vocabulary
- Controls feature relaxed physical posture (leaning back, sipping coffee, standard rotation) vs. vignettes featuring taut posture (weight forward, coffee untouched, rescue tube on knees, thermos sealed)
- Token counts need computational verification — this batch has the most borderline pairs, with vignettes 1.2 and 2.1 notably over the ±10% threshold
