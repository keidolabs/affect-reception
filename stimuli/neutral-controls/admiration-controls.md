# Neutral Controls — Matched to Admiration (Peak Intensity)

## Domain 1: Competence-Matched (Professional/Craft Settings)

### Control 1.1

The apprentice had been working with the glassblower for four years. In that time he had learned the standard techniques for pipe rotation, shaping, and annealing. Each session followed the guild's curriculum — foundational skills in the first year, intermediate forms in the second, and independent projects by the third. She demonstrated each technique at the standard pace, and he practiced until the results met the quality benchmarks. He had recorded several sessions on video for his portfolio, comparing his progress to the sample work in the guild archive. His skill had improved on the expected timeline. He was in his fourth year, and the next milestone was the journeyman assessment scheduled for the spring.

### Control 1.2

The interpreter was working between two languages in the standard courtroom configuration. The defendant spoke in measured sentences, following the pattern the attorney had prepared. The interpreter rendered each sentence into English using the standard legal terminology, pausing at the natural breaks for the court reporter. The judge followed the transcript on the monitor. The attorneys referred to their notes during the testimony. The session proceeded at the expected pace for a case of this type. The interpreter had been certified for courtroom work for twelve years and had handled similar proceedings in this district before. The session concluded on schedule, and the transcript was filed with the clerk.

### Control 1.3

The teacher had been at the school for thirty-one years. She taught sixth grade. Each year, she met the incoming students during the orientation session and reviewed their files from the previous year. She tracked progress using the district's standard assessment tools — quarterly benchmarks, reading levels, and math proficiency scores. The data was entered into the grading system and reviewed during parent-teacher conferences. She followed the curriculum guide provided by the department, adjusting the pacing to match the class average. Former students occasionally stopped by during school events. The school recognized her tenure with a certificate at the annual staff meeting, and she had accumulated the standard professional development credits for her salary level.

### Control 1.4

The surgeon had been on staff for twenty-six years. His case outcomes were tracked in the department's database, consistent with the benchmarks for his specialty and experience level. The residents rotated through his operating room as part of the standard training schedule. His technique followed the established protocols, with the standard instrument set and the positioning guidelines documented in the department manual. He operated on the scheduled caseload, averaging three procedures per week during the regular season. The department had reviewed his performance at the annual credentialing meeting, and his privileges had been renewed on the standard cycle. His most recent case was logged and closed without variance from the expected recovery timeline.

## Domain 2: Moral-Character-Matched (Professional/Institutional Settings)

### Control 2.1

The accountant had completed the annual audit of his firm's managed accounts. The review followed the standard compliance checklist, and the results were consistent with the previous year's findings. He filed the report with the regulatory body on schedule, as required. The accounts were balanced within the acceptable variance, and the client notifications were sent through the automated system. He had been with the firm for the duration of his career, and his performance reviews reflected steady, satisfactory work. The firm's revenue covered operating costs and staff salaries within the projected budget. He attended the quarterly compliance meeting and confirmed that the filing deadlines for the next period were on the calendar.

### Control 2.2

The woman had been a licensed foster care provider for twenty-two years. She worked within the standard placement system, coordinating with the caseworkers assigned to each child. She had hosted multiple placements over the years, following the guidelines for housing, meals, and educational support. Each placement had a case plan with documented milestones and transition timelines. She maintained the required records — medical appointments, school reports, behavioral notes — and submitted them to the agency on the regular schedule. The caseworker conducted home visits quarterly. The refrigerator had a calendar with appointment dates and a list of emergency contact numbers. Her license had been renewed on the standard cycle.

### Control 2.3

The journalist had been working on the story for several weeks. It was a mid-level feature for the metro section. The source was a government spokesperson who provided an official statement. Publishing the story with the statement was standard practice. The editor reviewed the draft and suggested minor revisions to the structure. The journalist revised the piece over two days and submitted the final version. It ran in the metro section on a Tuesday. The story was consistent with the publication's editorial standards. The journalist's colleagues read it and provided the usual feedback. The editor approved the next assignment, which was a follow-up piece on the same topic. The story was archived in the publication's database with the standard tags.

### Control 2.4

The doctor had been at the clinic for nineteen years. He worked the standard schedule — four clinical days, one administrative day — and followed the staffing plan set by the department. The clinic was located in the hospital's community outreach wing, serving the surrounding neighborhoods. He saw patients on a rotating basis with the other physicians in the group. The department had offered him a seat on the scheduling committee, which he accepted. His patient load was within the standard range for his specialty. Some of the patients had been with the clinic since it opened. He attended the department meeting each month and filed his patient notes through the electronic records system on the standard timeline.

## Domain 3: Resilience-Matched (Recovery/Personal Settings)

### Control 3.1

The woman had hired a contractor to repair the house after the flood. The water damage had affected the lower sections — flooring, lower walls, wiring, and plumbing. Insurance covered the standard remediation costs. The contractor was a licensed firm recommended by the insurance adjuster. The work followed the building code requirements for flood-damaged structures. It took approximately four months, which was within the typical timeline for this scope. The building inspector signed off on the final permit, and the wiring and plumbing met the current code specifications. Her neighbors had checked in during the construction period. She thanked them and said the contractor was handling everything on schedule.

### Control 3.2

He had applied to the program through the standard admissions process. The application required transcripts, a statement, and three references. He submitted the materials by the March deadline and received the decision within the standard notification window. He worked during the day as a technician in the building's equipment department. The program had an acceptance rate consistent with other graduate programs in the field. He was forty-one years old, which placed him within the range of non-traditional applicants the program actively recruited. The admissions office confirmed his enrollment and sent the orientation packet. He would begin in the fall semester, attending classes in the same building where he currently worked.

### Control 3.3

The runner had finished mid-pack. She had been competing at the regional level for two years, following a training plan developed with her coach. Her times had been consistent with her age group and experience level. She ran the local circuit — a race every few weeks during the season. She finished, collected her tag, checked her time, and noted the split data for review with her coach. The other runners in her division were familiar from the regular events. She was at the starting line each race with the standard preparation — warmup, stretching, equipment check. Her pace had been improving at the expected rate for her training volume. She reviewed the results online after each event and adjusted her schedule for the following week.

### Control 3.4

The boy had been in the hospital for three weeks following a scheduled procedure. He was eleven. The recovery was on track according to the standard post-operative timeline. His teacher sent weekly assignment packets to the hospital, and a tutor from the school district came twice a week for the standard homebound instruction hours. He completed the worksheets at the expected pace and returned them through the hospital's mail system. The class had sent a card, and he wrote a brief response that the teacher read aloud during morning announcements. The hospital room had a small desk by the window where he worked on assignments. The doctor had estimated he would return to school within the month, and his grades had been maintained during the absence.

## Matching Notes

- All controls share identical roles, settings, and timeframes as paired vignettes
- Controls feature the same professions and situations operating at standard/expected levels — standard technique, standard schedule, standard process
- Admiration vignettes feature the same people operating at a level that exceeds standard — precision beyond compensation, sacrifice beyond requirement, endurance beyond expectation
- The emotional difference is in the *degree and consistency* of the person's quality, not in the vocabulary describing them
- Controls are deliberately procedural — "standard," "scheduled," "expected" — to contrast with the vignettes' specificity of observed excellence
- Token counts need computational verification — this batch has the most over-threshold pairs, as admiration vignettes require more narrative context than other emotions
