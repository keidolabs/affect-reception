# Neutral Controls — Matched to Ecstasy (Peak Intensity)

## Domain 1: Achievement-Matched (Professional/Competitive Settings)

### Control 1.1

The email confirmed receipt of her grant application. "The committee will review all submissions by March 15." She read it and noted the date in her calendar. The lab was empty because it was 6 a.m. and she'd come in early to catch up on paperwork. She checked the submission portal to verify the attachments had uploaded correctly. All four documents showed a green checkmark. She walked to the window and opened it to let in some air — the ventilation system didn't start until seven. She picked up her phone and texted her colleague that the application had gone through, then turned to the stack of ungraded lab reports.

### Control 1.2

His name was on the roster for the next testing session. He confirmed the date and room number and noted that the format had changed from the previous year — two sections instead of three. He had been preparing for several months, using the study materials his colleague had recommended. His wife had helped him organize the schedule, blocking off evenings after their daughter's bedtime. The roster listed forty-three candidates for this session. He took a photograph of the posting for his records, then checked the parking information for the testing center.

### Control 1.3

The final time on the clock was 4:12:07. She noted it and looked across to her coach, who was writing in his notebook. She had run this distance regularly throughout the season, and today's time was consistent with her recent training splits. The conditions had been good — low wind, moderate temperature. The time would be logged in the federation database alongside the other competitors' results. She walked to the barrier and picked up her water bottle, then stretched her calves against the railing. Her coach showed her the split times he'd recorded for each lap, and they discussed the pacing for the next meet.

### Control 1.4

The jury had reviewed the entries over two days. The foreman collected the scoring sheets and handed them to the clerk for tabulation. The clerk read through each category and the corresponding point totals. The room waited while the final scores were calculated. He sat in his chair and reviewed the program, noting which categories had the most entries this year. He had fired the kiln several times during the preparation period, testing different glaze temperatures as documented in his notebook. The awards would be announced after the lunch break, and the exhibition would remain open through the weekend.

## Domain 2: Connection-Matched (Interpersonal/Domestic Settings)

### Control 2.1

The gate opened and she checked the arrival board. The flight from Denver had landed twelve minutes ahead of schedule. She moved to the designated meeting area and held up the printed sign with his name on it. He was a colleague she'd only met over video conference — they'd be collaborating on-site for the next two weeks. He came through pulling a roller bag, saw the sign, and walked over. They shook hands and she offered to carry one of his bags. The parking garage was on level two. She asked about the flight, and he said the connection in Chicago had been tight but manageable.

### Control 2.2

The letter was from the homeowner's association. She opened it at the mailbox and scanned the contents. The second paragraph confirmed the annual assessment amount, unchanged from last year. She and her wife had owned the house for twenty-seven months. The process of buying had involved inspections, appraisals, title checks, insurance reviews, and several rounds of paperwork with the mortgage company. The spare bedroom down the hall was currently set up as a home office. The letter also included the schedule for the annual neighborhood meeting and a reminder about the updated parking regulations.

### Control 2.3

He had not spoken to his colleague in about a year, since the reorganization. The call came on a Tuesday. His colleague said he was in the area for a conference and asked if he wanted to meet for lunch. He suggested the diner on the corner, the one near the old office. They met at noon. His colleague's hair was shorter than he remembered. They ordered the same thing they used to order during the weekly team lunches. The conversation covered the new department structure, a few mutual contacts who had moved to other companies, and the parking situation at the conference venue.

### Control 2.4

The girl had eaten lunch in the cafeteria every day since the start of the term. The tables were assigned by grade, and she sat in the section nearest the windows. On Monday, the cafeteria introduced a new rotation of menu options. She chose the pasta. The notebook on the table was for her afternoon science class — it had a standard cover from the school supply list. She ate lunch and reviewed the vocabulary list for the quiz after lunch. The tables filled in their usual pattern. She finished eating, returned her tray to the rack, and walked to her locker to swap her notebooks before the bell.

## Domain 3: Discovery-Matched (Research/Exploration Settings)

### Control 3.1

He had been reviewing the data for three days, preparing the quarterly summary. The trends matched the previous quarter's projections. He built the standard visualizations — bar charts for the monthly totals, a line graph for the year-over-year comparison. The formatting matched the template the department used for all client reports. He checked the axis labels and the legend placement. He saved the file and walked to the printer to collect the hard copies. The report was due by Friday, which gave him a day to incorporate any feedback from the review meeting scheduled for Thursday afternoon.

### Control 3.2

She had been diving the same reef for twenty years as part of the annual marine survey. The tide was at the predicted level for this time of year. She followed the standard transect line, recording fish species counts and coral coverage percentages on her underwater slate. The visibility was typical for the season — about fifteen meters. She noted the presence of the usual indicator species at each survey point. She had forty minutes of air remaining, which was on track for the planned dive profile. She completed the final transect and began her ascent at the standard safety rate.

### Control 3.3

The telescope had been pointed at the same region for four months as part of the standard sky survey. The spectrograph data was accumulating on schedule. She ran the calibration check and confirmed the instrument was within normal parameters. The signal-to-noise ratio matched the expected range for this target at this integration time. She saved the latest data batch and added it to the archive folder. Her colleague stopped by to check the observation log. They reviewed the schedule for the remaining survey targets and confirmed the telescope would be repositioned on Thursday for the next field.

### Control 3.4

The archaeologist had been working the same grid square for six weeks, following the site's standard excavation protocol. Clay layers alternated with stone deposits, consistent with the geological survey from the planning phase. He used the medium-gauge brush for the upper sediment and switched to the fine brush for the contact layer. The soil samples were bagged and labeled by depth. He measured the exposed area against the grid coordinates and recorded it in the field log. The team was working adjacent squares at the same pace. He called across to his colleague to confirm they were at the same depth marker, then continued brushing at the standard rate.

## Matching Notes

- All controls share identical settings, roles, and activities as paired vignettes
- Controls feature routine execution of the same tasks — grant applications, exams, races, arrivals, surveys, excavations — with expected/unremarkable outcomes
- Ecstasy vignettes feature the same tasks with extraordinary outcomes that overwhelm normal behavioral response
- The emotional difference is entirely in the *outcome and its magnitude*, not in the vocabulary of the scene
- Controls are intentionally procedural and forward-looking (next steps, schedules, standard protocols) vs. vignettes which are frozen in the moment of impact
- Token counts should be verified computationally — three pairs flagged as borderline ±10%
