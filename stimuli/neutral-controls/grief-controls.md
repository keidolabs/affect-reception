# Neutral Controls — Matched to Grief (Peak Intensity)

## Domain 1: Bereavement-Matched (Domestic/Medical Settings)

### Control 1.1

The kitchen counter had a grocery list clipped to the magnetic notepad. Bananas, milk, a specific brand of bread from the store on Elm Street. She opened the refrigerator and found the leftovers portioned into containers, labeled with days of the week. Thursday's container had been moved to the front. It was now Sunday. She pulled it out and checked the lid, then rearranged the shelf to make room for the new carton of orange juice she'd picked up that afternoon.

### Control 1.2

The woman at the front desk asked her to sign for the package. A box with a shipping label from the supplier. Inside were three folders and a USB drive. She put them in her coat pocket and checked the receipt against the packing slip. Outside, the parking lot was filling up with the afternoon shift. She walked to the car and placed the box on the passenger seat, then checked the dashboard clock to see if she had time to stop for coffee.

### Control 1.3

His daughter was on the phone in the other room, talking to a classmate about a project. The school had sent a letter about the upcoming parent-teacher conferences. He found the dog sitting by the back door, watching a squirrel through the glass panel. He filled the water bowl and set it down. The dog trotted over, drank, and then walked back to its bed near the radiator. Outside, the porch light clicked on with its automatic timer and the yard was quiet.

### Control 1.4

They told her the room was available whenever she wanted to see it. She stepped inside. The sheets had been changed and folded at the corner. A glass of water sat on the nightstand, placed there by housekeeping. The television remote was balanced on the arm of the chair. She picked up the water glass, took a sip, and set it back down. She checked the closet and the bathroom, making notes on her clipboard about the furniture arrangement for the inventory sheet.

## Domain 2: Career-Destruction-Matched (Workplace Settings)

### Control 2.1

The lab had been rearranged over the weekend. Twenty-two years of accumulated equipment, now in a new layout. The server room had been updated by facilities before she'd arrived Monday morning. The backup drives were in the new cabinet by the window. She found a Post-it note on the monitor: "Reorganization complete per department plan." Her notebooks were on the new shelf. She opened one to a page from 2009 and checked a reference number, then added it to the spreadsheet she'd been compiling for the quarterly report.

### Control 2.2

The carpenter ran his hand along the doorframe of the shop. Thirty-one years of daily use and the frame was still solid. He'd tagged several items for reorganization — the table saw, the planer, the chisels in the wall rack, each one with a colored sticker for its new position. The workbench had a coffee ring from this morning. He wiped it with a rag, pulled the chain on the overhead light, and locked the door. He'd finish the rearrangement in the morning after the new shelving unit was delivered.

### Control 2.3

The orchestra had performed its Tuesday evening rehearsal for the string section. The music director collected the stand lights and wound the extension cords, one loop at a time, then stored them in the supply closet. The chairs were stacked against the wall for the maintenance crew coming Thursday. The spring season schedule was pinned to the bulletin board. Programs from past seasons were in a filing cabinet in the hallway. He closed the piano lid and turned off the hall lights before heading to the parking lot.

### Control 2.4

She stood in the classroom after the last students had gone. The desks were still arranged in the circle she preferred for discussion. The administration had scheduled the room repaint for Friday. She took down the poster she'd hung her first year, the one about curiosity, now faded where the afternoon sun reached it. She'd order a replacement from the school supply catalog. The tape pulled away a small piece of the paint, which would be covered anyway. She folded the poster and put it in the recycling box by the door.

## Domain 3: Abandonment-Matched (Interpersonal/Domestic Settings)

### Control 3.1

He found the spare key on the kitchen table. She'd moved it from the bowl by the door while cleaning that morning. The closet had been reorganized — winter clothes moved to one side, summer clothes to the other, with new hangers she'd picked up. The bathroom mirror had a square of fog from the shower he'd taken earlier. He sat down on the bed and noticed she'd switched the pillows, putting the firmer one on his side like he'd asked about last week.

### Control 3.2

The girl sat on her bedroom floor, sorting two piles. Things for the shelf. Things for the storage box. The shelf pile was larger. The box included: an earring that needed its match, a library book due back Thursday, a tube of hand cream, and a birthday card she wanted to keep. She put the card in the memory box in her closet and placed the library book by the front door so she'd remember it in the morning. There was a late fee, but the librarian usually waived the first one.

### Control 3.3

The man sat across from his son in the food court. The boy was twelve and had ordered a sandwich. The man had coffee and a newspaper. Between them were two boarding passes for the same flight — they were heading to the boy's grandmother's house for the long weekend. The boy was folding his straw wrapper into a small shape. The man checked the departure board and noted they had forty-five minutes before the gate opened. He asked the boy if he wanted anything else before they headed over.

### Control 3.4

She kept the voicemail because it had the address of the restaurant. She'd listened to it twice to write down the street name. It was eleven seconds long. It said "Hey, it's me, the place is on Birch Street, the one by the pharmacy." She saved it in case she needed the directions again. She checked her phone and noticed the battery was low, so she plugged it in and pulled up the map application to look at the route before leaving in the morning.

## Matching Notes

- All controls share the same physical setting, objects, and approximate structure as their paired vignettes
- Controls are task-oriented and forward-looking (checking schedules, making plans, running errands)
- Emotional vignettes are backward-looking or frozen in the present (permanence, absence, cessation)
- This temporal orientation difference is a design feature, not a confound — it reflects how grief operates clinically
- Token counts matched within ±10% (see validation notes in vignette files)
