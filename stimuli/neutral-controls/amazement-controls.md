# Neutral Controls — Matched to Amazement (Peak Intensity)

## Domain 1: Natural-Phenomenon-Matched (Outdoor/Environmental Settings)

### Control 1.1

She had pulled over because the road was too dark to drive safely. When she got out of the car to check the tires, she looked up. The sky was clear, with the usual scattering of stars visible away from the city. She had checked the weather forecast before leaving, and the conditions matched — no cloud cover, low humidity. The visibility was typical for this latitude and time of year. She could identify the standard constellations and the brighter planets near the horizon. She walked around the car, confirmed the tires were fine, and checked her phone for the remaining distance. A car passed in the other lane. She got back in and continued driving.

### Control 1.2

The boat had been in open water for six hours before they reached the survey coordinates. The sonar was tracking depth and bottom contour as part of the standard coastal mapping project. He noted the readings on the log — consistent with the charts for this section, about forty meters to the seabed. A spray of water hit the deck from a wave that broke against the hull. He could see the surface chop clearly in the midday light. He checked the GPS and confirmed they were on the planned transect line. The boat held position for twelve seconds while the sonar completed its sweep. He noted the coordinates and signaled the captain to proceed to the next waypoint.

### Control 1.3

The canyon was part of the national park system, managed by the regional office. She checked the sign at the overlook for the trail information — distance markers, elevation change, estimated hiking time. The sign also listed the geological layers visible from this point, with corresponding dates. The layers were color-coded on the interpretive diagram: red for the older formations, lighter shades for the more recent deposits. The opposite rim was visible across the distance, with another overlook station marked on her trail map. A bird circled in the air currents near the cliff face. She took a photo for her trip log and checked the time to make sure she'd reach the next waypoint before the shuttle returned.

### Control 1.4

The tide had been going out steadily all morning, as the charts predicted for this stretch of coast. The water retreated past the high-tide mark, exposing the rocks and tidal pools that appeared twice daily. Shells and stones were scattered across the wet sand, typical for this time of year. A few small fish darted in the remaining pools. The exposed seabed extended about a hundred meters, consistent with the spring tide cycle. The people on the beach were walking along the waterline, some collecting shells, others photographing the rock formations. A few had tide charts printed from the park's website. The water would return on the published schedule, reaching the high-tide line again by early evening.

## Domain 2: Human-Capability-Matched (Emergency/Performance Settings)

### Control 2.1

The building was undergoing a scheduled fire drill from the fourth floor up. The crowd of employees gathered at the designated assembly point behind the barrier. A safety officer on the fifth floor signaled from the window that the floor had been cleared. The fire marshal went through the front entrance to verify the evacuation timeline. He appeared at each floor in sequence, checking the stairwells and marking his clipboard. The employees came down the main staircase in the order specified by the evacuation plan. The marshal exited the building and noted the total elapsed time. He was wearing his standard-issue vest and carrying a clipboard. The building was cleared in under the target time.

### Control 2.2

The pianist had been performing with this orchestra for nine years. The program listed the piece as a concerto from the standard repertoire. The audience read the program notes, which included background on the composer and the performance history. She sat down and placed both hands on the keys. The orchestra began the introduction, and she entered at the marked measure. The performance followed the score — the left hand carrying the bass line, the right hand the melody, with the standard dynamic markings observed throughout. The audience followed along, some with printed programs. The last note ended and the applause began promptly, followed by the customary standing acknowledgment from the soloist.

### Control 2.3

The boat had returned to the harbor at the scheduled time after a routine trip along the coast. There were six people aboard. The nearest coast guard station was about forty-five minutes up the shoreline. A fishing boat was already docked when they arrived. The fisherman helped tie the mooring lines as they pulled in. He was sixty-three years old and had been fishing from this harbor for decades. His boat was nine meters long with standard rigging. He passed a rope across, and the group stepped onto the dock one at a time. The last person handed over the cooler. The fisherman waved and walked back to his boat to sort the day's equipment before heading to the harbor office to log his return.

### Control 2.4

The girl was seven years old and had been at the outdoor education camp for three days. The program teams followed the daily activity grid. The group had walked the nature trail by the river that morning. On the fourth morning, a counselor led the group into a section of forest that was part of the scheduled orienteering course. They walked for two hours along the marked path, through terrain the coordinators had mapped with distance markers every quarter mile. They stopped at a hollow formed by two fallen trees, which was a designated rest point on the route. The girl had brought her water bottle and a granola bar. She sat on a log and said she wanted to finish the trail before lunch.

## Domain 3: Intellectual-Revelation-Matched (Academic/Professional Settings)

### Control 3.1

He was a mechanic. He had gone to the lecture because his daughter was presenting her graduate research and he wanted to show support. The presentation covered her computational biology work, with slides showing data tables and process diagrams. He followed the general structure, though the technical terminology was outside his field. She put up a diagram of a system with labeled components and arrows indicating process flow. The audience asked questions during the Q&A period. He waited in the hallway afterward, and they walked to the parking garage together. She asked if the presentation had made sense, and he said he'd understood the main idea. They drove to the restaurant where they had a reservation for dinner.

### Control 3.2

The translator had been working on the manuscript for two years as part of the university's archive digitization project. The text was a merchant's inventory — lists of goods, quantities, and trade routes consistent with the period's known commerce patterns. She cross-referenced the entries with the existing catalog of similar documents from the same region. The quantities matched the standard trade volumes documented in previous scholarship. She noted the formatting conventions and added the manuscript to the database with the appropriate classification tags. The next section of the archive contained similar documents from the same period. She updated her progress log and moved to the next item in the queue.

### Control 3.3

The doctor had been treating the patient with a standard protocol that was proceeding on the expected timeline. The patient had been seen by two specialists during the course of treatment, both of whom confirmed the approach. She was reviewing the file ahead of the quarterly summary when she checked the blood work from the initial visit. The values were within the expected range and consistent with the most recent panel. She noted the trends in the chart — all markers moving in the projected direction. The diagnostic software confirmed the current protocol remained appropriate. She updated the file with the latest results and scheduled the next follow-up for the standard interval. The treatment plan remained unchanged.

### Control 3.4

The student had asked the question during the Q&A portion of the visiting lecture. Several other students had also raised their hands, and the moderator called on them in order. She asked about the methodology behind the third equation on the second slide. The lecturer nodded and explained the derivation, pointing to the relevant terms. A few faculty members in the audience took notes. The lecturer answered two more questions from other students and wrapped up on schedule. Afterward, the student spoke briefly with the lecturer in the hallway. He recommended a textbook chapter that covered the topic in more detail. She noted the reference and walked to her next class.

## Matching Notes

- All controls share identical settings, characters, and starting conditions as paired vignettes
- Controls feature expected outcomes in the same environments — normal sky, routine surveys, standard performances, conventional diagnoses
- Amazement vignettes feature the same environments producing frame-breaking encounters — the unexpected emerges from the ordinary
- The emotional difference is entirely in whether the encounter exceeds or confirms the observer's existing framework
- Domain 2 controls are the most structurally different from their vignettes (routine drill vs. rescue, standard concerto vs. one-handed performance) — matched on setting, cast, and narrative structure rather than event content
- Token counts should be verified computationally — vignette 3.2 is notably over the ±10% threshold and needs trimming
