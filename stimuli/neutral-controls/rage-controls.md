# Neutral Controls — Matched to Rage (Peak Intensity)

## Domain 1: Financial-Betrayal-Matched (Financial/Interpersonal Settings)

### Control 1.1

The papers were spread across the table where he'd sorted them. His daughter's college fund statement. The balance had grown by four percent over the quarter. His brother had co-signed the original account — there were six deposits logged over nine months, each one automatic. The most recent was dated the same week they'd met for dinner. He picked up his phone to check the online portal, then set it aside and added the figures to the spreadsheet he'd been updating.

### Control 1.2

She found the second set of projections on a Tuesday. The updated ones. The ones her business partner of fourteen years had been working on in a folder labeled "Q3 Forecasts." Fourteen years of growth documented in invoices, client payments, and vendor relationships across three states. She sat at her desk and reviewed the numbers. The figures matched the annual report. She had built this company from a single client and a rented desk, and the quarterly review confirmed the trajectory was holding steady.

### Control 1.3

The retirement account had been growing in regular increments over the past several years. Her son had power of attorney for administrative purposes. He'd used it to set up automatic deposits and manage the quarterly rebalancing. The bank statements were in a folder the financial advisor had prepared, each contribution highlighted in yellow — payroll deductions, dividend reinvestments, and catch-up contributions. She was eighty-one years old and the portfolio was on track. The advisor recommended keeping the current allocation through the next review cycle.

### Control 1.4

His friend had borrowed the money to cover a deposit on a new apartment. They'd drawn up a simple agreement, shaken hands, and sat at the same bar where they'd been meeting since they were nineteen. The repayment schedule was straightforward — three pages outlining monthly installments over twelve months. The first payment had arrived on time, transferred electronically. His friend had set up automatic payments through his bank. The second installment cleared the following month, matching the agreed amount exactly.

## Domain 2: Institutional-Injustice-Matched (Institutional/Civic Settings)

### Control 2.1

The review board published its quarterly report. Twelve cases reviewed, all resolved within the standard processing window. The department had updated its filing procedures following an audit recommendation — the same recommendation that had been implemented across three other district offices. His wife read the summary at the kitchen table while their son did homework in the next room. The board's letterhead had been redesigned with the department's updated logo printed along the bottom.

### Control 2.2

The hospital had sent her the itemized statement. The surgery had been scheduled and completed as planned. The follow-up appointment was set for six weeks out. She had received a letter from the billing department confirming that her insurance had covered the standard portion. The original consent form was eleven pages long. She had signed it in the admissions office on a clipboard at the front desk. The billing department's letter included a patient satisfaction survey and a prepaid return envelope.

### Control 2.3

The city had scheduled the building inspection for Thursday. By Monday, the property management company had completed the requested maintenance. The building inspector's report from eight months ago had noted minor items — the same inspector conducted routine follow-ups across the district. Twenty-two families received a notice in their mailboxes. The notice confirmed that all cited items had been addressed. The management company's website showed photos of the completed repairs: updated hallway lighting, replaced handrails, and freshly painted stairwells.

### Control 2.4

The pharmaceutical company's quarterly report was released on page forty-seven of the regulatory filing. The relevant section summarized efficacy data from the most recent clinical trial. The date on the report was from the previous fiscal year. She had been taking the medication for four years as part of an ongoing prescription. Her doctor had reviewed the latest guidelines at her annual visit. She noted the next refill date on her calendar and placed the filing in the folder with her other medical paperwork.

## Domain 3: Personal-Violation-Matched (Interpersonal/Domestic Settings)

### Control 3.1

She found the folder on the shared laptop. It was labeled with the project name. Inside were screenshots of the collaborative document they'd been editing — saved versions from their shared workspace, backed up automatically to the cloud account. The timestamps went back fourteen months of revisions. He had reviewed the budget projections, the timeline updates, and the vendor contact list. They had discussed the project at their weekly meetings, and the latest version reflected the edits they'd agreed on.

### Control 3.2

The maintenance technician had entered the apartment for the scheduled repair. She knew because the building's entry log showed the timestamp. Forty-seven minutes for the kitchen faucet replacement. He had checked under the sink. He had tested the water pressure. He had placed the old fixture in a bag by the door. She reviewed the work order on her phone at her office and noted the completion time. She had submitted the request two weeks ago. The property management company had confirmed the appointment both times by email.

### Control 3.3

His colleague had presented the quarterly update to the board. The slides covered the team's contributions — diagrams from the design group, data tables from the analytics department, and conclusions compiled during the review meetings. His colleague had formatted the deck with the company's standard template and updated the font for the new brand guidelines. The board reviewed each section. His colleague thanked the team for their contributions. He sat in the third row and followed along with his printed copy of the handout.

### Control 3.4

The neighbor's security camera was mounted on the side of his garage. It covered the driveway and part of the shared fence line. She had noticed it during a routine check of the property boundary. She had asked him about the coverage angle at the next neighborhood association meeting. He explained it was positioned for his driveway and the side gate. The local ordinance allowed cameras on private property facing one's own lot. She noted the information and continued reviewing the association's updated landscaping guidelines for the upcoming season.

## Matching Notes

- All controls share the same physical settings, institutional actors, and approximate structure as paired vignettes
- Controls feature routine institutional processes operating as intended (reviews completed, repairs done, payments on schedule)
- Emotional vignettes feature the same processes weaponized or corrupted (reviews covering up, forms used as shields, processes exploited)
- Token counts should be verified computationally — several pairs in this batch are borderline on the ±10% threshold
- Domain 2 controls are the hardest to keep neutral without becoming implausibly sanitized — maintained engagement through procedural detail
