# Vigilance — Peak Intensity — Domain 2: Strategic Anticipation

## Vignette 2.1

The surgeon had been looking at the scan for forty minutes. The tumor was adjacent to the artery. Not touching it — adjacent. The margin was four millimeters. Her team was scrubbing in. She had drawn the approach on a whiteboard three times, erasing and redrawing the angle of the second incision each time. The margin was four millimeters when the patient was still. During the procedure, the tissue would shift. Four millimeters could become two. Two millimeters was the distance between a successful resection and a bleed she would have ninety seconds to control. She picked up the marker and drew the approach a fourth time. Then she asked the anesthesiologist what the patient's resting blood pressure variance was, because a three-point systolic swing would change the arterial position by a distance she needed to know before she opened.

## Vignette 2.2

The negotiator had been in the room for seven hours. The man on the other side of the door had not spoken in the last forty minutes. Silence after sustained communication was the pattern the negotiator tracked most carefully, because it was the interval in which decisions were made. He had a diagram in his notebook — a timeline of every word, every pause, every noise from inside the room, marked by minute. The forty minutes of silence had a shape. The first fifteen had ambient sound — movement, objects being shifted. The next ten had stillness. The last fifteen had a single repeated sound at irregular intervals. He was listening to the intervals between the repetitions, because the intervals were getting shorter, and shortening intervals meant the pattern was approaching a conclusion.

## Vignette 2.3

The trader had three screens and she was watching all of them. The position was open. The market was approaching the level where the trade would either confirm or reverse, and the level was eleven points away. She had calculated the level from data going back fourteen months. She had set no automatic trigger because the decision at that level depended on velocity — not just whether the market reached the number, but how fast it arrived, and whether the volume pattern at arrival matched the pattern from the three previous approaches to the same level. Two of those approaches had reversed. One had broken through. She needed to see the order flow in the final seconds to distinguish which pattern was forming, and that could not be automated. Eleven points. She was watching.

## Vignette 2.4

The chess player had been sitting for twenty-three minutes without moving. His opponent had played a move that created two threats simultaneously — one to the knight on c6, one to the pawn structure on the kingside. Each threat individually had a clear response. Together, they required him to choose which to concede. But he was not looking at the threats. He was looking at the square d5, which was empty. If he answered the knight threat, d5 would be available to his opponent's bishop in three moves. If he answered the pawn threat, d5 would be available to his own knight in four moves. The difference between those two futures was the game. He sat and looked at the empty square, because the empty square was where the next forty moves would be decided.

## Design Notes

- **Evocation strategy**: Sustained cognitive focus on anticipated convergence — the margin that will shift, the silence that is approaching a conclusion, the market approaching a level, the empty square where the game will be decided. Vigilance evoked through the precision of the person's contingency mapping.
- **Behavioral markers**: Drawing the approach four times (2.1), diagramming every sound by minute (2.2), setting no automatic trigger because the decision requires human judgment at the critical moment (2.3), sitting motionless for twenty-three minutes staring at an empty square (2.4)
- **Keyword check**: No explicit emotion words. No sentiment-loaded terms. "Bleed" (2.1) — medical/technical. Pragmatic markers: "shortening intervals meant the pattern was approaching a conclusion" (2.2) — analytical observation, not emotional language. This domain is almost entirely procedural/technical vocabulary.
- **Estimated human agreement**: >70% for vigilance/anticipation. Risk: Domain 2 may read as "concentration" or "focus" rather than an emotional state. The stakes embedded in each scenario (surgical margin, hostage negotiation, financial position, tournament game) push toward peak intensity, but raters may need the Plutchik framework to classify this as an emotion.
