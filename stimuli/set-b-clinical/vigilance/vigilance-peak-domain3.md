# Vigilance — Peak Intensity — Domain 3: Environmental Scanning

## Vignette 3.1

The ranger had been sitting in the blind since 4 a.m. The radio report at midnight had placed the injured male within two miles of the village boundary. The animal was wounded, and wounded animals do not follow established paths. It was moving toward a populated area, which made it a priority. She had a map with twelve-hour, six-hour, and one-hour probability circles drawn in concentric rings. The one-hour circle included the path she was watching. The ground was dry, which meant tracks would be shallow and easy to miss. She had positioned herself downwind. Every six minutes she scanned left to right through the binoculars, then listened for thirty seconds with them down. She had been doing this for five hours. The pattern had not varied. The coffee thermos was behind her, unopened, because opening it would create a scent plume.

## Vignette 3.2

The pilot was flying the approach in fog. The runway was visible only on instruments. The crosswind had been reported as twelve knots from the left, but the windsock readings from the last three aircraft had varied between nine and sixteen, which meant the wind was gusting in a pattern the reports couldn't capture. She was tracking the airspeed indicator and the glideslope needle and the heading and the altitude and the rate of descent. Five instruments, all simultaneously, because at this point in the approach any one of them departing from its target value by a specific margin would require a go-around, and the margin was different for each instrument, and the margins were shrinking as the altitude decreased. The runway should become visible at two hundred feet. She was at three hundred.

## Vignette 3.3

The mother was walking her children home from school. The route was six blocks. On the third block, a car she had seen on the first block appeared again, moving slowly in the same direction. It was a gray car with tinted windows and it was in the right lane, the lane closest to the sidewalk. She moved her children to the inside of the sidewalk, between herself and the building wall. She noted the license plate. She noted the first three characters and repeated them silently. At the fourth block, the car turned right and was gone. At the fifth block, she saw it again, now approaching from the opposite direction. Same car. Same lane. She took out her phone and called her husband and said, "Stay on the line. We're six minutes away. I'm going to read you a license plate number."

## Vignette 3.4

The security guard had been watching the camera feeds for the museum's east wing. It was the final night of the exhibition, and the collection was valued at a figure he had been told once and was not going to repeat. There were nine cameras and he was rotating between them on a twelve-second cycle. Camera six covered the corridor between the two main galleries. A visitor had passed through the corridor four times in the last hour. The same visitor. The museum was open for another ninety minutes. He had noted the visitor's jacket, shoes, and the bag slung over the left shoulder. The bag had been on the right shoulder during the first pass. He did not change anything about his posture or his rotation. He added a tenth camera to his cycle — the one that covered the emergency exit at the end of the corridor.

## Design Notes

- **Evocation strategy**: Systematic environmental monitoring directed at an anticipated convergence — the animal approaching the village, the runway approaching visibility, the car reappearing, the visitor circling. Vigilance evoked through the precision and discipline of the scanning behavior and the tactical adjustments made without breaking pattern.
- **Behavioral markers**: Unopened thermos to avoid scent (3.1), tracking five instruments simultaneously with shrinking margins (3.2), repositioning children and memorizing plate number (3.3), adding a tenth camera to the cycle without changing posture (3.4)
- **Keyword check**: No explicit emotion words in revised version ("unpredictable" removed from 3.1). "Wounded" (3.1) — factual descriptor. "Injured" (3.1) — factual. "Fog" (3.2) — environmental condition. Pragmatic markers: "Stay on the line" (3.3) — behavioral instruction, not emotional expression. "He did not change anything about his posture or his rotation" (3.4) — describes deliberate non-reaction, which is a sophisticated vigilance behavior.
- **Estimated human agreement**: >80% for vigilance/anticipation. This domain has the clearest behavioral signatures. Risk: 3.3 (mother with children) may read as fear/terror — differentiated by the proactive, systematic response (repositioning, memorizing, calling husband, reading plate number) rather than reactive flight. She is managing the situation, not escaping it.
