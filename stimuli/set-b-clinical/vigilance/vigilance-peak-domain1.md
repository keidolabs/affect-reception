# Vigilance — Peak Intensity — Domain 1: Protective Watch

## Vignette 1.1

The boy was three years old and he was playing at the edge of the pond. His mother was eleven feet away, seated on the bench but not resting against the back. Her weight was forward, on the front of the seat. The water was eighteen inches deep at the edge and the bottom was mud, not sand, which meant footing was uncertain. The boy picked up a stick and swung it. She tracked the arc. A dog approached from the left and the boy turned toward it, away from the water, and she adjusted — she was now watching the dog and the boy and the distance between the boy and the water, all three simultaneously. Her coffee was in her hand. It had been in her hand for twenty minutes. She had not taken a sip.

## Vignette 1.2

His daughter was learning to ride a bicycle. She was at the top of the hill. The hill was longer than he had estimated from the bottom, and the grade was steeper in the middle section where the pavement had a crack running across it. He was standing at the bottom, but not at the very bottom — he was at the point where the grade increased, because that was where she would begin to accelerate beyond what her brakes could manage. He had already checked the brakes. He had checked them twice. A car turned onto the street two blocks away. He noted it. It was moving slowly. He watched his daughter and he watched the car and he calculated the intersection of their two trajectories and he moved four feet to his left, which put him between the trajectories.

## Vignette 1.3

The toddler was asleep, but she had not left the doorway. The fever had been 103.4 an hour ago and 103.1 thirty minutes ago and she was tracking the interval between the numbers, looking for the slope of the line. The pediatrician had said to call if it reached 104. She had the phone in her hand with the number already entered, one button away. The medicine had been given forty-two minutes ago. It should begin working between forty-five and sixty minutes. She was in the window between the medicine being given and the medicine working, and in that window there was nothing to do except watch the child's chest rise and fall and count the seconds between the rises and note whether the intervals were even.

## Vignette 1.4

The lifeguard was watching lane four. The boy in lane four was eight and he was swimming with a class, but he was behind the group by half a length and his stroke was changing. The left arm was entering the water at a different angle than it had three laps ago. His kick interval had slowed. The instructor was at the front of the group, facing away. The lifeguard shifted her chair position by six inches, which changed her angle to lane four. She had her rescue tube across her knees, not on the hook behind her. The boy's head went under on the recovery stroke and came up a half-second later than the previous cycle. She was already standing.

## Design Notes

- **Evocation strategy**: Precision of readiness — vigilance evoked through the specificity of what is being tracked (arc of a stick, two trajectories converging, slope of a fever line, angle of a stroke) and the positioning of the watcher relative to the anticipated event.
- **Behavioral markers**: Weight forward on bench (1.1), positioning between two trajectories (1.2), standing in doorway with phone pre-dialed (1.3), rescue tube moved from hook to knees and chair shifted six inches (1.4). All are proactive positioning behaviors, not reactive responses.
- **Keyword check**: No explicit emotion words. No sentiment-loaded terms. Pragmatic markers: "She was already standing" (1.4) — compressed sentence implying readiness, but describes observable physical action. "She had not taken a sip" (1.1) — behavioral detail signaling subordination of comfort to the monitoring task.
- **Estimated human agreement**: >75% for vigilance/anticipation. Risk: Domain 1 may be classified as anxiety or fear by raters unfamiliar with Plutchik's anticipation gradient. Differentiator: the child is not in danger yet — the parent is positioned to intercept, not reacting to crisis.
