# Rage — Peak Intensity — Domain 2: Institutional Injustice

## Vignette 2.1

The review board published its findings. No misconduct found. The officer was back on patrol the following Tuesday. The dashcam footage had been classified as inadmissible due to a filing error — the same filing error that had occurred in three previous complaints against the same officer, processed by the same clerk. His wife read the report at the kitchen table while their son ate cereal in the next room with his arm still in a cast. The board's letterhead had a motto about service and community printed along the bottom.

## Vignette 2.2

The hospital had billed her for the full amount. The surgery that used the wrong implant. The surgery that required a second surgery. The second surgery that left her with a limp she would have for the rest of her life. The hospital's attorney sent a letter stating that the signed consent form covered all procedural variations. The form was eleven pages long. She had signed it in a hallway on a clipboard while being wheeled to the operating room. The attorney's letter was dated the same day the hospital had nominated itself for a patient care excellence award.

## Vignette 2.3

The city had condemned the building on a Thursday. By Monday, the developer's company had filed for the lot. The building inspector's report cited violations that hadn't existed during the previous inspection eight months ago — the same inspector, the same building, different findings. Twenty-two families received a notice taped to the front door. The notice gave them thirty days. The developer's website already showed a rendering of what would go on the lot: glass and steel, a rooftop pool, units starting at a price that none of the twenty-two families would recognize as a monthly number.

## Vignette 2.4

The pharmaceutical company's internal emails were released on page forty-seven of the court filing. The relevant line: "Efficacy data does not support label claims at current dosing. Recommend: adjust marketing language, not dosing." The date on the email was six years before the recall. She had taken the medication for four of those six years. Her doctor had prescribed it based on the label. She counted backward. Her daughter had been conceived during year two. She looked at her daughter. She looked at the filing. She looked at her daughter.

## Design Notes

- **Evocation strategy**: The gap between institutional language and lived reality. Official processes (review boards, consent forms, inspections, regulatory filings) used as instruments of violation. The evidence is in the documentation itself — dates, patterns, procedural "errors" that repeat.
- **Behavioral markers**: Reading a report at a kitchen table, counting backward, looking between a child and a document, recognizing a pattern in filing errors
- **Keyword check**: No explicit emotion words. Flagged: "wrong" (2.2 — "wrong implant," factual/descriptive, not evaluative). "Condemned" (2.3 — legal term). Pragmatic markers: the juxtaposition of institutional self-congratulation with harm (award nomination + billing for botched surgery, community motto + misconduct clearance).
- **Estimated human agreement**: >80% for rage. Risk: 2.4 could read as terror (threat to child) — the corporate knowledge/cover-up framing should anchor in violation rather than threat. Monitor in human validation.
