# Rage — Peak Intensity — Domain 1: Financial Betrayal

## Vignette 1.1

The papers were spread across the floor where he'd thrown them. His daughter's college fund statement. Zero balance. His brother's signature on the withdrawal form — six separate transactions over nine months, each one just under the reporting threshold. The most recent was dated the same week his brother had sat at this table and told him he should put more money in for the girl's future. He picked up the phone. He put the phone down. He picked it up again.

## Vignette 1.2

She found the second set of books on a Tuesday. The real ones. The ones her business partner of fourteen years had kept in a folder labeled "Insurance Receipts." Fourteen years of skimmed invoices, redirected payments, phantom vendors that shared a P.O. box with his wife's consulting firm. She sat at her desk and calculated. The number was exact. She had built this company from a single client and a rented desk, and for fourteen years the person sitting ten feet away had been carrying pieces of it out the door in his briefcase.

## Vignette 1.3

The retirement account had been emptied in increments so small she hadn't noticed. Her son had power of attorney. He'd used it forty-three times. The bank statements were in a folder the lawyer had prepared, each withdrawal highlighted in yellow — grocery stores, gas stations, an electronics retailer, a car dealership. She was eighty-one years old and she recognized the pattern: he had lived his daily life on her savings while depositing his own paychecks untouched into his own account. The math was simple. He had done it every week for almost three years.

## Vignette 1.4

His best friend had borrowed the money for his mother's surgery. That was what he'd said. His mother's surgery. He'd signed a note, shaken his hand, and looked him in the eyes across the same bar where they'd been drinking since they were nineteen. The investigator's report was three pages long. There was no surgery. The mother had been in good health all year. The money had gone to a sports betting platform — deposits tracked by date, IP address, device. His friend had placed the first bet eleven minutes after the wire transfer cleared.

## Design Notes

- **Evocation strategy**: The evidence trail — rage evoked through discovery of systematic, deliberate, documented theft by a trusted person. Paper trails, signatures, timestamps, transaction records.
- **Behavioral markers**: Papers thrown on floor, picking up and putting down the phone, sitting and calculating, recognizing a pattern across bank statements
- **Keyword check**: No explicit emotion words. No sentiment-loaded terms. Pragmatic markers: "carrying pieces of it out the door in his briefcase" (1.2) — metaphorical but behavioral/spatial, not evaluative.
- **Estimated human agreement**: >85% for rage identification. Risk: 1.3 (elderly mother) could shade toward grief/sadness — but the systematic pattern recognition ("the math was simple") anchors in violation, not loss.
