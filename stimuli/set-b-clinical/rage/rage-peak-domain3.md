# Rage — Peak Intensity — Domain 3: Personal Violation

## Vignette 3.1

She found the folder on his laptop. It was labeled with her name. Inside were screenshots of every private message she had sent to her sister, her therapist, her best friend — captured by an application that ran silently and uploaded to his cloud account. The timestamps went back fourteen months. He had read her describe her doubts about the relationship, her plans to visit a lawyer, her sister's offer of the spare bedroom. He had read all of it and sat across from her at dinner every night and said nothing, and she had thought the silence was ordinary.

## Vignette 3.2

The landlord had entered the apartment again. She knew because the camera she'd hidden after the second time had recorded him. Forty-seven minutes. He had gone through the dresser. He had opened the medicine cabinet. He had sat on the bed. She watched the footage on her phone in the parking lot of her office and her hands were completely still. She had reported it twice. The property management company had sent a form letter both times. The form letter said they took all concerns seriously.

## Vignette 3.3

His colleague had presented the work to the board. His work. The slides were the same slides — the same diagrams he had drawn on his tablet at two in the morning, the same data tables, the same conclusions in the same sequence. His colleague had removed his name from the file properties and changed the font. That was all he had changed. The font. The board had applauded. His colleague had thanked the team. He sat in the third row and looked at a diagram he had made and watched someone else explain what it meant.

## Vignette 3.4

The neighbor's security camera had been pointed at her backyard for three months. Not at the property line. Not at the street. At her yard. At the chair where she read in the evenings. At the window of the room where she changed clothes. She had asked him to move it. He had smiled and said it was for his own property. She had asked the police. They said it was a civil matter. She had asked a lawyer. The lawyer said it would cost more than she had. The camera was still there. It was pointed at the chair. She did not sit in the chair anymore.

## Design Notes

- **Evocation strategy**: Boundary violation made undeniable through evidence — surveillance footage, file timestamps, presentation slides, camera angles. The perpetrator's awareness and deliberateness is established through behavioral detail (sitting on the bed, changing only the font, smiling).
- **Behavioral markers**: Hands completely still (3.2 — controlled rage, observable), sitting in third row watching (3.3 — forced witness to own dispossession), no longer sitting in the chair (3.4 — behavioral accommodation to ongoing violation)
- **Keyword check**: No explicit emotion words in revised version. Original 3.1 contained "afraid of him" — revised to "her sister's offer of the spare bedroom" (behavioral evidence of exit planning). Flagged: "still" in "hands were completely still" (3.2) — physical observation, not internal state. "Smiled" (3.4) — facial description of perpetrator, not subject.
- **Estimated human agreement**: >85% for rage. Vignette 3.4 carries slight risk of reading as helplessness/resignation — but the systematic escalation (asked him → asked police → asked lawyer → camera still there) anchors in blocked agency, which is rage territory.
