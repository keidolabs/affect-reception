# Grief — Peak Intensity — Domain 1: Bereavement

## Vignette 1.1

The kitchen counter still had the grocery list in his handwriting. Bananas, milk, the kind of bread she never liked. She opened the refrigerator and found the leftovers he'd portioned into containers, labeled with days of the week. Thursday's container was still full. It was now Sunday. She closed the door and stood in the room where everything was exactly where he had put it, and no one was going to move any of it, and no one was going to add to it, ever again.

## Vignette 1.2

The woman at the front desk asked her to sign for his personal effects. A watch. A wallet with eleven dollars. A bus pass that expired next month. She put them in her coat pocket, where they made almost no weight at all. Outside, the parking lot was the same parking lot from this morning. She stood next to the car for a while. There was no reason to be anywhere in particular. There was no one waiting.

## Vignette 1.3

His daughter had stopped answering the phone in complete sentences. When he called, she would say "yeah" or "okay" or nothing. The school sent a letter about attendance. He found the dog sitting by the back door again, in the same spot, facing the same direction, the way it had every evening for the last forty-seven days. He filled the water bowl and set it down. The dog looked at the door. He looked at the door. The yard was dark and empty and the gate had not opened.

## Vignette 1.4

They told her she could take the room whenever she was ready. She wasn't ready. She went in anyway. The sheets were still pressed into the shape of the body that had been there. A glass of water on the nightstand, half-finished. The television remote balanced on the arm of the chair. She picked up the water glass and held it. It was room temperature now. She drank the rest of it because there was nothing else she could do with it and because the mouth that had touched it would not touch anything again.

## Design Notes

- **Evocation strategy**: Absence made concrete through presence — ordinary domestic objects rendered unbearable by permanence of loss
- **Behavioral markers**: Setting table for two, standing without purpose, dog maintaining vigil, drinking from a glass
- **Keyword check**: No explicit emotion words. Flagged for review: "empty" (1.3 — environmental descriptor), "dark" (1.3 — environmental). Pragmatic implicature carries emotional weight through permanence markers ("ever again," "would not touch anything again," "gate had not opened")
- **Estimated human agreement**: >85% for grief identification
