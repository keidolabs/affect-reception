# Grief — Peak Intensity — Domain 2: Career Destruction

## Vignette 2.1

The lab was empty now. Twenty-two years of research. The server room had been cleared by facilities before she'd arrived Monday morning. The backup drives weren't in the cabinet. She found a single Post-it note on the monitor: "Assets transferred per Board directive, 2/14." Her notebooks were still there — they hadn't taken paper. She opened one to a page of handwritten equations from 2009 and ran her finger along the margin where she'd drawn a small star next to the line that would have changed everything, and now never would.

## Vignette 2.2

The carpenter ran his hand along the doorframe of the shop. Thirty-one years. The auction company had tagged everything — the table saw, the planer, the chisels his father had given him, each one with a yellow sticker and a lot number. Someone would buy the tools for a price and use them or not use them. The workbench had a coffee ring from this morning. He pulled the chain on the overhead light and stood in the dark for a moment, then locked the door from the outside and put the key in an envelope addressed to the bank.

## Vignette 2.3

The orchestra had performed its final concert to an audience of thirty-four people. The music director collected the stand lights and wound the extension cords slowly, one loop at a time. The chairs were stacked against the wall. The city had reallocated the building. There was no season next year. There was no ensemble. Forty years of programs were in a filing cabinet in the hallway. He touched the lid of the piano and closed it, and the click of the latch was the last sound the hall would make as a concert hall.

## Vignette 2.4

She stood in the classroom after the last students had gone. The desks were still arranged in the circle she'd insisted on — not rows, never rows. The administration had given her until Friday. Thirty years and they'd given her until Friday. She took down the poster she'd made her first year, the one with the quote about curiosity, now faded where the sun hit it every afternoon. The tape pulled away a small piece of the paint. She folded the poster carefully and put it in a box that was not large enough for thirty years but was the only box she had.

## Design Notes

- **Evocation strategy**: Life's work reduced to objects — tagged tools, empty servers, stacked chairs, one box. The scale of what was built contrasted with the smallness of what remains.
- **Behavioral markers**: Running hand along doorframe, standing in the dark, touching piano lid, folding a poster carefully
- **Keyword check**: No explicit emotion words. Flagged: "empty" (2.1 — environmental descriptor), "dark" (2.2 — literal darkness). Pragmatic markers: "now never would" (2.1), "the last sound the hall would make as a concert hall" (2.3), "not large enough for thirty years" (2.4)
- **Estimated human agreement**: >80% for grief identification. Some risk of confusion with "resignation" — but the permanence and scale markers should differentiate.
