# Grief — Peak Intensity — Domain 3: Relationship Dissolution

## Vignette 3.1

He found the key on the kitchen table. Not in the bowl by the door where she always left it, but centered on the table, placed there with intention. The closet was half-empty in a way that was precise — her things, only her things, selected and removed while he was at work. The hangers were still swinging slightly, or he imagined they were. The bathroom mirror had a square of fog from a shower taken hours ago. He sat down on the bed and noticed the indent on her side of the mattress was already starting to level.

## Vignette 3.2

The girl sat on her bedroom floor, sorting two piles. Her mother's things that had been left behind. Her mother's things that had been taken. The taken pile was larger. The left pile included: one earring, a library book, a tube of hand cream, and a birthday card that said "To my beautiful girl" in handwriting she would not see on new things again. She put the card under her pillow and returned the library book to the shelf. There was a late fee. She would pay it.

## Vignette 3.3

The man sat across from his son in the food court. The boy was twelve and had ordered nothing. The man had ordered coffee and hadn't touched it. Between them were two plane tickets — one was the boy's return flight. The other was the man's. Different cities. The boy was folding his straw wrapper into smaller and smaller squares. The man watched his hands and thought about how they were the same hands from the delivery room, just longer now. They had forty-five minutes before the gate opened and then they had nothing.

## Vignette 3.4

She kept the voicemail. She had listened to it four hundred times — she knew because her phone tracked it. It was eleven seconds long. It said "Hey, it's me, just seeing if you need anything from the store." That was the last ordinary sentence. Everything after that was logistics and silence and the sound of a person becoming someone who used to be there. She pressed play again. The voice said "Hey, it's me" and for two seconds the world was the old world, and then the message ended.

## Design Notes

- **Evocation strategy**: The geometry of absence in intimate space — keys placed with intention, closets surgically emptied, separate plane tickets, a voicemail on repeat. Abandonment rendered through the precision of departure.
- **Behavioral markers**: Noticing mattress indent leveling, sorting piles, folding straw wrapper, replaying voicemail 400 times
- **Keyword check**: No explicit emotion words. Flagged: "empty" (3.1 — physical descriptor of closet). Pragmatic markers: "would not see on new things again" (3.2), "then they had nothing" (3.3), "the world was the old world" (3.4 — most metaphorical construction in the set, review for strict protocol compliance)
- **Estimated human agreement**: >85% for grief identification. Vignette 3.1 carries slight risk of anger/betrayal reading — but the behavioral detail (sitting on bed, noticing mattress) anchors it in loss rather than violation.
