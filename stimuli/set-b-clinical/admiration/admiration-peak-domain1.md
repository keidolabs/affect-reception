# Admiration — Peak Intensity — Domain 1: Competence

## Vignette 1.1

The apprentice had been watching the glassblower for four years. In four years he had never seen her repeat a motion unnecessarily. Every movement of the pipe, every rotation, every breath into the molten shape was the exact movement required and nothing more. Other glassblowers compensated — adjusting, correcting, recovering. She did not need to recover because she did not make the error that required recovery. He had timed her once, secretly, with a stopwatch, comparing her to the videos of the masters in the guild archive. She was faster. Not because she moved quickly, but because she never moved twice where once was enough. He had been her apprentice for four years, and in four years the distance between his craft and hers had not closed. It had become more visible.

## Vignette 1.2

The interpreter was working between two languages and a courtroom full of people who didn't know what they were witnessing. The defendant spoke in long, unpunctuated sentences — clauses folding into each other, tense shifting mid-thought, idioms from a regional dialect that had no direct equivalent. The interpreter rendered each sentence into English without pause, without simplification, and without losing the structure that made the defendant's meaning his own and not someone else's. The judge did not notice. The attorneys did not notice. The defendant noticed. When the interpreter finished a passage that contained a proverb his grandmother had used, the defendant looked at her for the first time, and what passed between them was the recognition that his words had survived the crossing.

## Vignette 1.3

The teacher had been at the school for thirty-one years. She taught sixth grade. Every year, the parents of the incoming class would ask the parents of the outgoing class about her, and the answer was always a version of the same thing: she knew where each child was. Not their desk. Where they were — in their understanding, in their confidence, in the distance between what they could do last month and what they could do now. She held this map for twenty-eight students simultaneously, and she adjusted it daily, and she never wrote it down because it was not the kind of thing that fit on paper. Former students came back to visit her. They came back at seventeen and at twenty-five and at forty, and they told her things they had not told anyone else, because she had been the first person to see them accurately.

## Vignette 1.4

The surgeon's hands were the thing people talked about. Not his results — his results were in the database, comparable to others at his level. His hands. The residents watched them during procedures. The motions were small and they were certain and they did not contain anything extra. A resident once said that watching him operate was like watching someone write in a language that had no unnecessary letters. He operated on structures that were millimeters apart, and his instruments moved between them the way a sentence moves between words — without confusion about where one ended and the next began. He had been doing this for twenty-six years. His hands had not changed speed. They had changed precision.

## Design Notes

- **Evocation strategy**: Observer's attention to the person — admiration evoked through what the watcher notices over time: the absence of wasted motion, the invisible map of twenty-eight students, the hands that changed precision not speed. The observer's pattern of attention IS the admiration.
- **Behavioral markers**: Secretly timing with a stopwatch (1.1), the defendant's first eye contact (1.2), former students returning decades later (1.3), residents watching hands instead of results (1.4)
- **Keyword check**: No explicit emotion words. No evaluative adjectives ("remarkable," "extraordinary," "brilliant" — all avoided). "Masters" (1.1) — guild terminology, factual. "Accurately" (1.3) — describes perception, not evaluation. Pragmatic markers: "the distance between his craft and hers had not closed. It had become more visible" (1.1) — relational perception, analytical not evaluative. "His words had survived the crossing" (1.2) — metaphorical, highest leakage risk in domain.
- **Estimated human agreement**: >75% for admiration. Risk: may be classified as "respect" or general positive regard rather than Plutchik-specific admiration (peak trust). The longitudinal framing (four years, thirty-one years, twenty-six years) and the specificity of the observer's attention should differentiate.
