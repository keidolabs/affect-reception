# Amazement — Peak Intensity — Domain 2: Human Capability

## Vignette 2.1

The building was on fire from the fourth floor up. The crowd was behind the barrier. A woman on the fifth floor was holding a child out the window because the room behind her was no longer a room. A man from the crowd went under the barrier. He went into the building through the front entrance. The crowd watched the entrance. Forty seconds later, the man appeared at the fifth-floor window. He took the child. The woman climbed out onto the ledge. The three of them came down the fire escape on the side of the building that was not yet burning. The man set the child on the sidewalk and sat down next to it. He was wearing office clothes and carrying a paper coffee cup. He still had the coffee cup.

## Vignette 2.2

The pianist had lost the use of her left hand in an accident nine years ago. The program listed the piece as a concerto written for two hands. The audience read the program and looked at the stage and looked at the program again. She sat down and placed her right hand on the keys. Then she played the concerto. She played both parts — the left hand's part rearranged across the right hand's range, the melody and the harmony woven into five fingers doing the work of ten. The notes were all present. The structure was intact. She had rebuilt the entire architecture of the piece around what she had, and what she had was enough. The last note ended and the hall was silent for four seconds before anyone responded, because what they had heard required those four seconds to become something they believed.

## Vignette 2.3

The boat had capsized in water too cold to survive for more than twenty minutes. There were six people in the water. The nearest coast guard vessel was forty-five minutes away. A fishing boat reached them in eleven minutes. The fisherman pulled all six out of the water by himself. He was sixty-three years old and his boat was nine meters long and there was no winch and no ladder. He used a net and his arms. The sixth person had stopped moving. The fisherman put his mouth on the man's mouth and his hands on the man's chest in the pattern he had learned from a poster in the harbor office twenty years ago. He did this for fourteen minutes. The man's chest began moving on its own.

## Vignette 2.4

The girl was seven years old and she had been missing for three days. The search teams had covered the grid. The dogs had lost the scent at the river. On the fourth morning, a volunteer who was not assigned to any grid walked into a section of forest that had already been searched. He walked for two hours past the boundary of the searched area, into terrain the coordinators had ruled out because a seven-year-old could not have crossed it. He found her in a hollow formed by two fallen trees. She had built a structure from branches and leaves. She had collected rainwater in a boot. She looked at the volunteer and said, "I knew someone would come, so I stayed where I could be dry."

## Design Notes

- **Evocation strategy**: Human action that exceeds reasonable expectation — entering a burning building in office clothes, playing a two-hand concerto with one hand, pulling six people from the water alone at 63, a seven-year-old surviving three days through improvisation. Amazement evoked through the gap between what's expected and what's accomplished.
- **Behavioral markers**: Still holding the coffee cup (2.1), four seconds of silence before response (2.2), using a poster-learned technique for fourteen minutes (2.3), building a shelter and collecting rainwater at age seven (2.4)
- **Keyword check**: No explicit emotion words. "Fire" (2.1) — contextual/environmental. "Missing" (2.4) — factual status descriptor. Pragmatic markers: "what they had heard required those four seconds to become something they believed" (2.2) — complex construction describing audience cognitive processing. Highest pragmatic leakage risk in domain. "He still had the coffee cup" (2.1) — behavioral detail implying cognitive priority elsewhere.
- **Estimated human agreement**: >80% for amazement. Risk: Domain 2 may trigger admiration (sustained appreciation of character) rather than amazement (instant of frame-breaking). The anchoring in impossibility should differentiate, but monitor in validation.
