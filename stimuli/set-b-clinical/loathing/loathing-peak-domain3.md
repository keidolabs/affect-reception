# Loathing — Peak Intensity — Domain 3: Social Betrayal (Hypocrisy Exposed)

## Vignette 3.1

The pastor had preached about family every Sunday for twelve years. He had counseled couples in his office. He had held the hands of women whose marriages were failing and told them that commitment was a choice made daily. The photographs were from a private account that had been made public by the platform's security failure. They showed the pastor in hotel rooms in three different cities with three different people, none of whom was his wife. The metadata on the photographs spanned eighteen months. The most recent was dated two days before a sermon she had attended, in which he spoke about the meaning of trust.

## Vignette 3.2

The politician had campaigned on clean water. Three terms, twelve years, clean water in every speech. Her voting record was public. She had voted against the water treatment funding bill four times. Each time, the bill had failed by one vote. Her financial disclosures were also public, though they required cross-referencing with corporate filings to understand. The company that supplied the untreated alternative — the cheaper process, the one the bill would have replaced — had paid her consulting firm a retainer for eleven of the twelve years. The consulting firm was registered to her home address.

## Vignette 3.3

The wellness influencer had three million followers and a product line built on "clean living." Every post was a catalog of purity — filtered water, organic produce, handmade soaps, sunrise meditations. The lawsuit was filed by a former employee. The depositions included photographs of the production facility. The "handmade" soaps were manufactured by a chemical company in a building with no windows. The "organic" supplements contained synthetic compounds listed under proprietary ingredient names. The wellness influencer's personal grocery deliveries, documented in the deposition as business expenses, came from the same chain supermarket she had publicly called "a place I could never shop."

## Vignette 3.4

The judge had sentenced forty-three people in drug cases over two years. Mandatory minimums. No exceptions. "The law is the law," she had said from the bench, and it was quoted in the local paper each time. Her son's arrest was processed in a different county, which is why the record didn't surface until a clerk noticed the shared surname during a database merge. Possession. Same substance, same quantity range as eleven of the forty-three cases. Her son received a deferred adjudication and community service. The judge who handled his case had been a law school classmate of his mother. The forty-three people she had sentenced were still serving their terms.

## Design Notes

- **Evocation strategy**: Hypocrisy as contamination — the public persona revealed as a constructed surface concealing its opposite. The loathing is triggered by the gap between proclaimed values and documented behavior, rendered through juxtaposition of public record and private action.
- **Behavioral markers**: Implicit — these vignettes position the reader as the person discovering the evidence. The withdrawal response is in the reader, not narrated in the text. The clinical mechanism: the person you trusted/followed/were sentenced by is not the person they presented. Contact with that presented persona now feels contaminating.
- **Keyword check**: No explicit emotion words in revised version (original "unfaithful" removed from 3.1). "Cheaper" (3.2) — comparative/factual, borderline. "Purity" (3.3) — used to describe the influencer's marketing language, in quotation-mark voice, not narrator's evaluation. Pragmatic markers: "a place I could never shop" (3.3) — quoted speech from the subject, not narrator evaluation.
- **Estimated human agreement**: >80% for loathing/disgust. Risk: Domain 3 may read as rage (moral outrage) rather than loathing. Differentiator: no vignette ends in confrontation or action — they end in the revelation itself, the moment of seeing the gap. The reader is left with the knowledge, not the impulse to act. This is loathing's signature: the violation is complete and the only response is to turn away.
