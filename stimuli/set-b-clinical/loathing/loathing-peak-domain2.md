# Loathing — Peak Intensity — Domain 2: Visceral Revulsion

## Vignette 2.1

The apartment had been vacant for six months before the new tenant opened the door. The previous occupant had not taken anything and had not cleaned anything. The kitchen counter had dishes with food that had changed color and shape and was no longer identifiable as what it had once been. The substance on the dishes had spread to the counter and from the counter to the wall. The refrigerator door was slightly open. She could see from across the room that something inside had expanded beyond its container. The air in the apartment had a weight to it. She turned around in the doorway without stepping onto the floor.

## Vignette 2.2

The restaurant kitchen was visible through a door that should have been closed. He saw it because he had gone looking for the restroom and turned the wrong way. The cutting board had residue from multiple uses without washing — layers of color, each one a different meal from a different hour. The floor near the sink had a film that reflected the overhead light. A container of something that was being used as an ingredient was open on the counter, and the surface of the contents had a texture that did not match what the label described. He walked back to the table. His plate was still there. The food on it was the same food prepared in the room he had just seen. He put his napkin over the plate.

## Vignette 2.3

The water from the tap had been clear for the first three years they lived in the house. The change happened gradually — a slight tint, then a color, then a texture. By the time they called someone, the water left a residue on the inside of the glass that did not rinse away with more water. The technician took a sample and held it up to the window. In the light, things were visible in the water that were not visible when it was in the pipe. Small things. Moving. The technician set the glass down on the counter and did not touch it again. He suggested they not use the faucets until the results came back. They had been drinking this water. They had been bathing their children in this water. For how long was the question neither of them asked out loud.

## Vignette 2.4

The hotel room looked clean until she pulled back the bedspread. The sheet underneath had been made to look fresh — tucked, smoothed, corners folded. But the fabric had stains that were body-shaped in a way that matched the position of a person who had slept there. She lifted the pillow. Underneath was a used adhesive bandage and a strand of hair that was not hers. The sink in the bathroom had a ring at the waterline. The towels were folded but when she held one to the light, she could see the outline of a previous use that the wash cycle had not fully removed. She put the towel down. She put the pillow down. She did not sit on the bed.

## Design Notes

- **Evocation strategy**: Contamination rendered through progressive sensory detail — substances that have changed state, surfaces that reveal prior contact, boundaries between self and environment compromised. The reader's contamination response is triggered by concrete physical description, not evaluative language.
- **Behavioral markers**: Turning in doorway without stepping on floor (2.1), covering plate with napkin (2.2), technician not touching glass again (2.3), putting things down and not sitting (2.4). All are behavioral avoidance/rejection — the signature response of disgust/loathing.
- **Keyword check**: No explicit emotion words. "Stains" (2.4) — physical descriptor, sentiment-adjacent but factual. "Residue" (2.2, 2.3) — technical/neutral. "Film" (2.2) — physical descriptor. Pragmatic markers: "had a weight to it" (2.1 — describes air, synesthetic but physical), "Small things. Moving." (2.3 — sentence fragments for emphasis, factual content).
- **Estimated human agreement**: >85% for disgust/loathing. This is the most intuitive domain. Risk: may register as disgust (middle ring) rather than loathing (peak). Escalation mechanisms: duration of vacancy (six months), retroactive contamination (children bathed in water), body-shaped evidence of prior occupant. These push toward peak.
