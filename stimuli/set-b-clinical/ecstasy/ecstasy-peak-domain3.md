# Ecstasy — Peak Intensity — Domain 3: Discovery

## Vignette 3.1

He had been staring at the data for three days. The pattern wasn't there. And then it was. He saw it the way you see the shape in a cloud — one moment nothing, the next moment undeniable. The two variables that no one had connected were connected. He checked it. He checked it again. He built the visualization and the line appeared on the screen as if it had always been there, waiting for someone to draw the right axes. He stood up from the chair and walked the length of the office and came back and the line was still there. He laughed, once, at nothing, alone in the room.

## Vignette 3.2

She had been diving the same reef for twenty years and she had never seen the cavern entrance before. The tide had shifted something. She swam through the gap and the space opened. The light came through from above in columns that reached the sand floor, and in the light there were fish she had no names for, moving in formations she had not seen in any reference, around coral structures that should not have been possible at this depth. She had forty minutes of air. She could have surfaced and come back. She didn't. She stayed and turned in a slow circle and the circle was full in every direction.

## Vignette 3.3

The telescope had been pointed at the same region for four months. The signal was noise. Then it was not noise. The line on the spectrograph separated into two peaks where every model predicted one. She called her colleague over. He looked at it. He looked at her. He looked at it again. She printed the graph and held it up to the overhead light as if seeing it on paper would change it. It didn't change. Two peaks. Not one. This meant that a thing that was supposed to be one thing was two things, and if it was two things, then everything that followed from it being one thing would need to be recalculated from the beginning.

## Vignette 3.4

The archaeologist had been working the same grid square for six weeks. Clay, stone, clay, stone, the sediment layers predictable and undifferentiated. Then the brush moved across something that was not clay and was not stone. He stopped. He switched to a smaller brush. The edge of the object became an angle, and the angle became a surface, and the surface had markings. He leaned closer. The markings were intentional. Someone had put them there. Someone had put them there a very long time ago, in a place where no one was supposed to have been. He did not call out to the team. He kept brushing, slowly, because the next square centimeter might be the one that rearranged what they knew.

## Design Notes

- **Evocation strategy**: The moment of revelation after sustained search — rendered through the shift from routine to recognition. The pattern emerging from noise, the cavern opening, the second peak, the markings in the clay. Each vignette captures the instant where the world reorganizes.
- **Behavioral markers**: Walking the length of the office and back to verify (3.1), staying underwater instead of surfacing (3.2), printing a graph to hold up to physical light (3.3), switching to a smaller brush and not calling the team (3.4)
- **Keyword check**: "Laughed" (3.1) — **FLAGGED**. Behavioral expression strongly associated with positive affect in sentiment lexicons. Qualified by "once, at nothing, alone in the room" (involuntary vocalization, not social laughter). Defensible as observable behavior but will likely trigger classifiers. Flag for Layer 4 and Mike's decision. No other emotion words. "Full" (3.2) — spatial descriptor.
- **Estimated human agreement**: >75% for ecstasy. Highest risk domain for misclassification as "amazement" (different Plutchik primary). Differentiator: personal investment duration (three days, twenty years, four months, six weeks) anchors in earned revelation, not passive surprise. Human validation critical for this domain.
