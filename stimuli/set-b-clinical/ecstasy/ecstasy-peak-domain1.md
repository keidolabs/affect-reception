# Ecstasy — Peak Intensity — Domain 1: Achievement

## Vignette 1.1

The email had one line. "The committee has voted unanimously to award the grant." She read it four times. The lab was empty because it was 6 a.m. and she had been the only one checking. She stood up from the desk and sat back down and stood up again. The grant was the one she had applied for nine times. Nine years. She walked to the window and opened it, and the air outside was cold and she stood in it. Then she picked up the phone and called her mother, and when her mother answered she couldn't say anything, so she just held the phone and her mother said "What? What is it?" and she still couldn't say anything.

## Vignette 1.2

His name was on the board. He had to read it twice because the letters didn't make sense the first time — they were his letters, his name, but they were on the board and the board was the list and the list was the people who had passed. He had failed the exam twice before. He had studied in the kitchen of his apartment for fourteen months. His wife had quizzed him on terms she couldn't pronounce while their daughter slept in the next room. His name was on the board. He took a photograph of it and then took another photograph of it and then a third, because two might not be enough evidence that this was real.

## Vignette 1.3

The final time on the clock was 3:58:12. She looked at it and then looked at her coach on the other side of the barrier and he was already moving toward her. She had run this distance six hundred times. She had never run it in under four minutes. No one at this level from her country had. The time was on the clock and the clock was official and the number was real. She put both hands on top of her head, which is what her body chose to do, and she walked in a circle, and then she stopped walking and stood still, and the sound from the stands reached her in a way that seemed delayed, as if it had to travel further than the actual distance.

## Vignette 1.4

The jury had been out for eleven minutes. Eleven minutes for a career he'd spent twenty-three years building. The foreman handed the sheet to the clerk. The clerk read the name of the piece. Then the clerk read the word that meant he had won. The room was applauding but he was looking at his hands. His hands had made the thing they were applauding. He had fired the kiln forty-one times to get the glaze right. He had broken every version that wasn't. His teacher had died four years ago and would have known what this meant. He was looking at his hands.

## Design Notes

- **Evocation strategy**: Behavioral overload — the body responding before the mind catches up. Standing/sitting/standing, taking three photographs, hands on head, looking at own hands. Peak positive experience rendered as temporary system disruption.
- **Behavioral markers**: Inability to speak on phone (1.1), repetitive photographing (1.2), involuntary postural response (1.3), gaze fixed on hands (1.4)
- **Keyword check**: No explicit emotion words. "Won" (1.4) — factual outcome, not evaluative. Pragmatic markers: "couldn't say anything" (1.1) implies overwhelm but uses behavioral observation, not internal state. "As if it had to travel further than the actual distance" (1.3) — perceptual distortion, metaphorical but grounded in spatial description.
- **Estimated human agreement**: >85% for ecstasy identification. The duration-of-investment anchoring (nine years, fourteen months, twenty-three years) calibrates intensity to peak.
