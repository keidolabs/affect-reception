# Terror — Peak Intensity — Domain 2: Medical Crisis

## Vignette 2.1

The doctor had stopped talking and was looking at the scan on the screen. She was looking at the doctor. He tilted the screen slightly away from her, which he had not done during the first three images. He picked up the phone and dialed a three-digit extension. He said "Can you come to Room 4" and nothing else. He hung up. He had not turned back to her. He was writing something. She could see the scan from where she sat — a white shape in a field of gray, in a place where the other images had shown only gray. The second doctor arrived in less than two minutes.

## Vignette 2.2

The nurse had pressed the button above the bed and then pressed it again. That was two presses. He knew what two presses meant because his wife had been on this floor for nine days and he had learned the codes from watching the nurses. One press was routine. Two was urgent. Three was the one he did not think about. The nurse was checking the IV line and moving faster than she had moved all week. Another nurse came through the door already pulling on gloves. His wife's eyes were open but she was looking at a point on the ceiling that was not the television and was not him and was not anything.

## Vignette 2.3

She was twenty-six weeks along and the liquid on the bathroom floor was not what it was supposed to be. The color was wrong. She sat on the edge of the bathtub and looked at it. She could feel the baby move, which meant the baby was still moving, which was the only fact she had. Her phone was in the kitchen. The kitchen was down the stairs. She stood up and the room changed shape for a moment and then came back. She held the doorframe. The car keys were on the hook by the front door. The hospital was eleven minutes away. She knew it was eleven minutes because she had driven it twice already as practice.

## Vignette 2.4

The allergist had said the reaction would be mild. That was what the allergist had said. The boy's lips had changed shape in the first ninety seconds. His father was holding him and looking for the nurse who had been there a moment ago. The hallway was empty. He looked at the boy's neck. The skin was different than it had been when they walked in. He looked at the desk. No one. He looked at the door at the end of the hall. He could not remember if it led to the main corridor or to a storage room. The boy in his arms was making a sound that was not his normal sound.

## Design Notes

- **Evocation strategy**: Medical environments where professional calm breaks — doctors who stop talking, nurses who move faster than usual, codes learned through observation. Terror evoked through the gap between what should happen (routine) and what is happening (deviation from protocol).
- **Behavioral markers**: Doctor tilting screen away, nurse pressing button twice, spatial scanning for absent help, holding the doorframe, father looking between child and exits
- **Keyword check**: No explicit emotion words. Flagged: "urgent" (2.2 — embedded in hospital code system the character has learned, technical not emotional. Borderline — flagged for Mike's review). "Wrong" (2.3 — factual observation about color). "Empty" (2.4 — spatial descriptor of hallway).
- **Estimated human agreement**: >85% for terror. Risk: 2.1 could read as anticipatory anxiety rather than terror — but the second doctor arriving in under two minutes anchors the immediacy.
