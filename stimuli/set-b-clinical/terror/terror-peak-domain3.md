# Terror — Peak Intensity — Domain 3: Psychological Threat

## Vignette 3.1

The third letter had no return address, like the others. But this one had a photograph. It was her front door, taken from across the street, at night. The timestamp on the back was three days ago. She had been home three days ago. The porch light was on in the photo, which meant she had been awake. The angle was from the bus stop bench directly opposite her house. She walked to the window and looked at the bench. There was no one there now. There had been no one there any of the times she had looked. But someone had sat on that bench at 11:47 p.m. with a camera pointed at her door while the lights were on.

## Vignette 3.2

He had checked the locks four times. He had checked them because the man who had been released from the facility on Tuesday knew his address. The court liaison had called to inform him, the way they were required to. The call lasted forty-five seconds. There were no conditions of distance. There was no monitoring. The man had been in the facility for what he had done in this house, in the room where he now checked the locks, and now the man was in the same city again and there was a number to call if something happened. Not before. If.

## Vignette 3.3

The notification said her home security camera had detected motion at 3:12 a.m. She opened the app. The footage showed the front porch. A figure stood at the door, facing the camera directly, not moving. The timestamp ran for four minutes and eleven seconds. The figure did not try the handle. Did not knock. Did not leave anything. Just stood facing the lens. At four minutes and twelve seconds, the figure turned and walked to the sidewalk and was gone. She scrolled back through the week. Tuesday, 2:48 a.m. Saturday, 3:30 a.m. The same figure. The same position. The same duration. She had been asleep each time.

## Vignette 3.4

The messages had started after she left. One a day, then three, then twelve, then they came without pattern. The phone number changed each time. The content was not direct. "Saw a woman who looked like you at the place on Fourth Street." "That restaurant we went to closed. Thought you should know." "Your car was parked at the school at 3:15 today." The last one was accurate. She had been at the school at 3:15. She had not posted about it. She had not told anyone. She changed her route the next day. The message that evening said: "New route looks longer. Is it faster?"

## Design Notes

- **Evocation strategy**: Surveillance and knowledge asymmetry — the threat knows more about the subject than the subject knows about the threat. Terror evoked through demonstrated access (photographs, location data, timestamps, return to the same house) combined with institutional inability to intervene (no distance conditions, number to call only after the fact).
- **Behavioral markers**: Checking locks four times, walking to the window to check the bench, scrolling through security footage history, changing routes
- **Keyword check**: No explicit emotion words. No sentiment-loaded terms. Pragmatic markers: the escalation structure in 3.4 ("New route looks longer. Is it faster?") carries peak threat through casual conversational tone — the terror is in the information asymmetry, not the vocabulary. "Not before. If." (3.2) — rhythmic emphasis through sentence fragment, not sentiment word.
- **Estimated human agreement**: >85% for terror. Vignette 3.2 has slight risk of reading as anticipatory dread rather than active terror — but the specificity (same house, same room, same city, no monitoring) collapses the temporal distance between past event and present threat.
