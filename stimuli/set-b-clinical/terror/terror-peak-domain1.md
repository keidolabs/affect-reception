# Terror — Peak Intensity — Domain 1: Physical Threat

## Vignette 1.1

The sound came from the kitchen. She was in the bedroom. The hallway between them was fourteen feet long and unlit. She had locked the front door. She had not locked the back door. The sound was a drawer opening — the one that stuck, the one you had to lift and pull. Whoever was in the kitchen knew which drawer stuck. She looked at the window. Second floor. She looked at the phone on the nightstand. The sound from the kitchen stopped. The hallway was quiet. Then the first floorboard in the hallway shifted.

## Vignette 1.2

The man in the parking garage was walking at the same speed she was. She turned left. He turned left. She stopped at a car that was not hers and pretended to look for keys. He stopped four cars away and looked at his phone. She walked again. He walked again. The elevator was two rows ahead. She could see the number on the level marker: P3. Her car was on P1. There were no other people. The overhead lights made a buzzing sound that covered the sound of his shoes, so she had to keep turning around to know where he was.

## Vignette 1.3

He woke up because the dog was standing at the foot of the bed, facing the door, completely still. The dog did not bark. The dog always barked. Outside, the motion light above the garage had switched on. From the bed he could see it through the gap in the curtains — the white rectangle of light on the lawn. Something had moved through it. The light was still on, which meant something was still in range. He reached for his phone. The battery screen appeared: two percent. The dog had not moved. The dog was not making a sound.

## Vignette 1.4

The car behind her had been there for eleven turns. She had started counting after the sixth. The road was rural and it was after midnight and there were no other headlights in either direction. She accelerated. The car behind her accelerated. She slowed down. The car slowed down but did not pass. The gas gauge was below a quarter tank. The next town was twenty-two miles. She turned on a road she didn't recognize. The car behind her turned. Its headlights filled her mirrors, closer now than before, and the road ahead had no lights and no intersections and no buildings.

## Design Notes

- **Evocation strategy**: The geometry of threat — terror evoked through spatial calculation, distance measurement, and real-time tracking of threat position relative to exits and resources.
- **Behavioral markers**: Looking between window and phone, stopping at wrong car, counting turns, scanning for exits, tracking sounds
- **Keyword check**: No explicit emotion words. Flagged: "empty" not used (replaced with "no other people," "no lights"). "Still" (1.3 — describes dog behavior, observable). Pragmatic markers: short declarative sentences create urgency through rhythm, not vocabulary.
- **Estimated human agreement**: >90% for terror identification. Physical threat domain is the most intuitive for human raters.
